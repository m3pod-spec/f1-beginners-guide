THE TWO BIG PRIZES

ONE SPORT, TWO CHAMPIONSHIPS

01 · DRIVERS

THE WORLD DRIVERS' CHAMPIONSHIP

Awarded to the most successful driver across the season. Drivers earn points from individual Grand Prix results, and the title is formally presented at the FIA Prize Giving Ceremony after the season.

02 · CONSTRUCTORS

THE WORLD CONSTRUCTORS' CHAMPIONSHIP

Awarded to the most successful constructor. A constructor is responsible for the intellectual rights to both the car's chassis and its engine. Since 1979, points scored by every car entered by a constructor have counted towards its total. Before that, only its highest-scoring driver counted.

A FEW UNUSUAL FIRSTS

THE DETAILS BEHIND THE TROPHIES

1958: Vanwall became the first Constructors' Champion. The championship was first awarded as the International Cup for Formula One Manufacturers; the name changed to World Constructors' Championship in 1981.

1966: Jack Brabham became the only driver to win the Drivers' Championship in a car of his own construction.

1969: Jackie Stewart won in a Matra entered by Ken Tyrrell's privateer team, the only Drivers' Champion in the supplied record to win in a car entered by a privateer team.

1964 and 1988: John Surtees and Ayrton Senna each won the title despite scoring fewer points overall than the runner-up, because of the points systems used at the time.

2002: Schumacher clinched the championship with six races remaining, the earliest clinch in the supplied record.

Championship geography: All but 17 Constructors' titles have been won by chassis designed and built in the United Kingdom: 16 by Ferrari and one by Matra were the exceptions.

THE CONSTRUCTORS' LEGACY

MORE THAN JUST THE DRIVER

The Constructors' Championship began in 1958. Ferrari has won it 16 times, McLaren 10, and Williams and Mercedes nine each. Ferrari also leads the supplied engine-manufacturer record with 16 titles, followed by Mercedes with 13, Renault with 12, Ford with 10 and Honda with six. Across the championship's history, 15 of the 170 chassis constructors that have entered a Grand Prix have won the title.

The Drivers' title has not always gone to a driver from the team that won the Constructors' title: on 12 occasions, the Constructors' Champion did not contain that season's Drivers' Champion. All but one Constructors' title in the supplied record went to a works team; the 1969 Matra title was entered by Ken Tyrrell's Matra International.
