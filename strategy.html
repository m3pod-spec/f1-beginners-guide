THE RACE WITHIN THE RACE

THINK FAST. PLAN SMART.

Speed matters, but so does timing. Teams make decisions throughout a Grand Prix based on tyres, traffic, weather and changing conditions.

01 · TYRES

Grip versus durability

F1 dry-weather tyres come in different compounds. Softer compounds generally offer more grip but wear faster; harder compounds usually last longer but may be slower over a lap.

02 · PIT STOPS

A planned pause

Drivers enter the pit lane so the team can change tyres or make permitted repairs. A fast stop matters, but choosing the right lap to stop can matter even more.

03 · THE UNDERCUT

Stop earlier to gain time

A team may pit its driver before a rival. Fresh tyres can produce faster laps, potentially allowing the driver to get ahead when the rival makes their own stop.

04 · SAFETY CARS

When the plan changes

A Safety Car neutralises the race while officials manage an incident. Cars slow down and bunch up, which can make a pit stop less costly in time — but the timing is never guaranteed.
