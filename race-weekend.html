HOW A RACE WEEKEND WORKS

A Grand Prix weekend has a rhythm. Sessions help teams prepare, drivers qualify for their starting spots, and then it all comes down to the race.

01 · PRACTICE

Learn the track

Practice sessions give drivers time to learn the circuit, test car settings and compare tyre behaviour. Teams use the data to decide what might work best for qualifying and the race.

02 · QUALIFYING

Fight for a starting spot

Qualifying usually has three knockout parts: Q1, Q2 and Q3. The slowest drivers are eliminated in the first two parts, and the remaining drivers fight for pole position in Q3.

03 · GRAND PRIX

The main event

The race starts from the grid. Drivers complete a set number of laps, make strategic decisions and try to finish as high as possible. The first driver to complete the race distance wins.

04 · SPRINTS

A shorter race format

Some weekends include a Sprint: a shorter race that awards points to the top finishers. Sprint weekends use a different session schedule, so check the event timetable.

✳ PADDOCK NOTE
qualifying usually sets the starting order, but penalties or other decisions can change the final grid.
